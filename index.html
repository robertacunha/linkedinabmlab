Stage 1 · Weeks 1 to 4

Set up and target list

An audit, tracking and a shared definition of a qualified account come first. Marketing and sales then rank the accounts, split the list once and map the buying roles. The baseline taken here anchors every later read.

Foundation

Weeks 1 to 3.

Audit

Start from what is running

  • Active LinkedIn campaigns, audiences, spend and results, last 6 to 12 months
  • CRM: how accounts, contacts and opportunities are recorded and linked
  • Other channels touching the same accounts (search, email, events, sales outreach)
  • The company's own promises, asks and proof points, collected from the site
  • Won and lost deals, last 12 months, for the Fit filter
Tracking

Account-level measurement

  • Insight Tag on every page; conversions for key pages and forms
  • Conversions API connected to the CRM (lead, opportunity, closed-won)
  • Revenue Attribution Report connected
  • Contacts rolled up to the company record; lead-to-account matching in place
  • Tier and arm fields on every CRM account, filled at the split; lead forms synced to the CRM
  • Legal basis confirmed before any contact list sync
ICP and defaults

What every test holds fixed

  • ICP: [industries], [size], [functions], [seniority]
  • Default hook [plain statement of the problem] and offer [product with the broadest problem]
  • Default reason to believe, format and ask: [neutral versions]
  • Default ABM message per buying role, live until the core set is approved
Sales agreement

Service level agreement and qualification rule

  • Qualification rule (MQA) agreed in writing; details in Run
  • Who acts when: marketing until qualified, then the account owner
  • Sales follows up within one working day of a reply, lead form or demo request
  • Shared definitions: engaged account, MQA, opportunity, pipeline
  • Where sales uses Sales Navigator: account lists per tier with test and control marked, alerts into the standup
Baseline

The 90 days before the first ad

  • Accounts on site and accounts where two or more people from the same company engaged
  • MQA rate, opportunity rate and pipeline per account
  • Days to opportunity; win rate and deal size from the last 12 months
  • Pulled per account, within tier and industry; compared by arm after the split
Launch checklist

Settings and assets before launch

  • Audience Expansion off
  • LinkedIn Audience Network off
  • Manual or cost cap bidding
  • Budget optimization off where spend must stay even across tests
  • Optimized ad rotation off on learning rounds
  • Company lists built with LinkedIn page URLs, match rate 80% or more
  • Thought Leader Ad approvals; one landing page per offer

Per learning round

Stage 2 · Week 4 to window end · Engine 2

Run

The ABM layer runs on the test arm from week 4, switches to the core set once Build is approved and runs one full sales cycle (at least 90 days). Both arms see the ICP campaigns; only the test arm sees the ABM campaigns. That exclusion is the holdout. The control arm keeps normal sales activity, and both arms are on the monthly scorecard.

Keep the control clean. Check weekly that control accounts are excluded from every ABM ad set and sales play, and never move accounts out of the control.

Qualify and hand off

Live from the first lead form, about week 10.

Qualification rule

When an account becomes an MQA

Engagement from several people at the account crosses the threshold agreed with sales in Set up.

Act now: [a director requests a meeting; two people view the demo].

Excluded: partners, competitors, poor fit.

Signals: page visits and post engagement from the account, visits to [pricing, demo, key pages], event registrations, a new executive in a relevant role, Sales Navigator buyer intent, third-party intent.

Outreach

By tier

  • Tier 1: personal messages from the account owner
  • Tiers 2 and 3, test arm only: a sequence per role
  • Meeting booked: the account’s other sequences pause
Handoff

Four points

  • Who replied, their role and what they said
  • Which signal or content moved them
  • Who else at the account engaged, and how
  • The agreed next step and its date

Logged in the CRM on the company record.

Stage 3 · Weeks 4 to 13 · Engine 1

Learn: Flight 1

Six rounds on the ICP layer, eighteen ads. Each round changes one element and runs at least three weeks, in A and B pairs only on an uploaded company list split in two. A cell wins when it leads on engaged target accounts and sales confirms the conversations; no clear leader is logged as a result. Answers feed the learning log and Flight 2.

Rounds 1 and 2

Cold ICP audience. Paired on list A and list B when the ICP is an uploaded list, in sequence otherwise. Objective: engagement. CTA: Learn more.

Rounds 3 and 4

ICP engagers over 90 days (ads, video, documents, site and page visitors), split by list A and list B. Objective: engagement and website visits. Below 300 per half, run in sequence (about three weeks more).

Rounds 5 and 6

Engaged pool and key page visitors over 30 days, with lead forms. Qualification and handoff go live here, around week 10. Below 300 per half, run in sequence and Build moves back about three weeks.

Learning log

One line per round, filled when it closes and confirmed with sales. This table is the brief for Build.

Stage 4 · Weeks 12 to 14

Build: Flight 2

One core message from Flight 1: the lever, offer, reason to believe, format, ask and situation that won. Written per buying role, never naming the account. It replaces the default messages on the ABM layer and refreshes the Thought Leader Ad.

Gate to the core set
  • Core set approved: one message per role and the refreshed Thought Leader Ad
  • Every claim, offer and promise checked against the company's own pages
  • Role audiences rechecked, each still clearing 300 members
Stage 5 · Window end, about week 27

Readout and scale

The test arm against the control arm on opportunity rate and pipeline per account, then one decision. Tier 1 is reported on its own.

The decision at window end

Scale
  • Test arm clearly ahead on opportunity rate or pipeline per account
  • Sales confirms the conversations
  • The extra pipeline pays back the ABM spend within the payback threshold
  • Extend the ABM layer to the control arm and the next accounts on the ranked list
Adjust
  • Engagement up, pipeline flat
  • The ads work; the handoff, the offer or the rule does not
  • Fix qualification and handoff, keep the split, run a second window
Stop or redesign
  • No difference after a full window
  • Go back to the learning log and the account list
  • Run a new Flight 1 round where the gap is

Once a deal closes, the same method runs on current clients to cross-sell other products.
