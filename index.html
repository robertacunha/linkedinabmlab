LinkedIn ABM Lab

A LinkedIn account based program in five stages, from the account list to pipeline. Two test engines run inside it: learning rounds on the ICP layer find what the buying committee responds to, and an account holdout on the ABM layer proves whether the program moves target accounts. Stages 1 to 4 end in a gate, so the next one only starts on solid ground.

System

Five stages, two test engines

The same sequence for any B2B brand. Only the brand, the accounts, the offers and the copy change. Stages 1 to 3 run in order, and each of stages 1 to 4 ends in a gate. Stage 4 runs as one weekly and monthly loop for a full sales cycle, and Stage 5 is the single decision at the end. The timing assumes a sales cycle of about three months and moves with it.

Engine 1 · ICP layer · Stage 2

Learning rounds: what does the committee value?

Six rounds, three cells each, one variable per round. Rounds are independent: every element that is not being tested stays at a neutral house version set in Set up, never at an earlier winner. That keeps each read clean, lets rounds run in parallel on split audiences, and fills a learning log instead of a chain of winners. The ICP layer has the volume a creative test needs; target accounts in both arms see it, so it also warms them up.

Engine 2 · ABM layer · Run and readout

Account holdout: does the program move accounts?

Before launch, the target list is split at random within tier and industry into a test arm and a control arm. Of the split accounts, only the test arm gets the ABM layer and the ABM sales plays. After one sales cycle, the two arms are compared on opportunity rate and pipeline per account. Account audiences are too small for creative A/B tests, so the ABM layer measures account impact.

Split list→Baseline→Treat test arm→Compare after one sales cycle
Test map

What is tested, and what simply runs

Three kinds of test answer a question: the six learning rounds, the holdout and the optional expansion round. Flight 1 rounds run in pairs on two random halves of the audience where each half clears LinkedIn's audience floor, otherwise one after the other, at least three weeks each. LinkedIn serves an ad set only when 300 matched members remain after all targeting, location included; every audience on this page is built to clear it.

How rounds are run and read

Three cells, one ad set, rotated evenly

LinkedIn's built-in A/B test compares two ad sets, runs at least two weeks and works best on large audiences. A learning round instead runs three ads in one ad set with rotation set to rotate evenly, at least three weeks. The format round is the exception: one format per ad set, so it runs three ad sets with the same audience and equal budgets.

When a round can be read

At least three weeks and roughly 100 clicks per cell for a click read. A cell leads when the gap is clear and holds over the last two weeks. Reads are directional: a cell is called when it leads on the primary read and sales confirms the conversations behind it. No clear leader is also an answer, and the log says so.

Read each format against its own normal

Thought Leader Ads are read and reacted to in the feed with few clicks to a page; video is watched without clicking. Compare a cell with cells of the same format, and use clicks to the landing page and engaged roles per account as the read.

Stage 1 · Weeks 1 to 4

Set up

Nothing goes live before the account can be tracked, the settings are safe, and sales has agreed what a qualified account is. Sales then picks the accounts, the list is split once and frozen, and the buying roles are mapped. The baseline taken here is what every later read is compared with.

Foundation

Weeks 1 to 3.

Audit

Start from what is running

  • Active LinkedIn campaigns, audiences, spend and results, last 6 to 12 months
  • CRM: how accounts, contacts and opportunities are recorded and linked
  • Other channels touching the same accounts (search, email, events, sales outreach)
  • The company's own promises, asks and proof points, collected from the site
Tracking

Account-level measurement

  • Insight Tag on every page; conversions for key pages and forms
  • Conversions API connected to the CRM (lead, opportunity, closed-won)
  • Revenue Attribution Report connected
  • Contacts rolled up to the company record; lead-to-account matching in place
Launch checklist

Safe settings, every ad set

  • Audience Expansion off
  • LinkedIn Audience Network off
  • Manual or cost cap bidding, Maximum Delivery off
  • Budget optimization off where spend must stay even across tests
  • Recheck after every edit: defaults can reset

The holdout split

Done once, before launch, and never changed during the window. The control arm keeps everything the company already does, so the comparison isolates the ABM layer.

Test arm
  • Half of tier 2 and tier 3 accounts, assigned at random within each tier and industry
  • Gets the ABM layer and the ABM sales plays in Run
  • Also sees the ICP layer like every ICP account
Control arm
  • The other half, matched on the baseline
  • Excluded from every ABM ad set and ABM play
  • Sees the ICP layer during Learn, then only the always-on post; normal sales activity
  • Never moved out when it shows intent
Outside the test
  • Tier 1 accounts: the tier 1 track, from week 2
  • Existing customers: the optional expansion round
  • Accounts already late-stage at the split: sales leads

Size rule. A clean holdout needs about 200 accounts or more per arm, so 400 or more tier 2 and tier 3 accounts. Most B2B target lists are smaller, which makes the fallback the usual path: with a smaller list, use matched lookalike ICP accounts as the control plus the baseline, or a stepped rollout (wave 1 starts, wave 2 waits and serves as control until it starts), and report the result as directional.

Four to six people per tier 1 account, two or three per tier 2, one or two per tier 3. The blocker stays on the map and out of the first wave.

Tier 1 track

Starts in week 2 and runs alongside every stage. Tier 1 never waits for the core set.

Weeks 2 to 4

Account plan

One page per account: priorities, the committee by name and role, open initiatives, the situation the account is in, and the one next step. Written with the account owner.

From week 4

People first

Human-written outreach per stakeholder, the expert post in the feed, and invitations to events or roundtables. One sender per stakeholder.

From week 14

Ads join

The core set runs to tier 1, clustered with test-arm or non-target ICP accounts to clear 300, never with control accounts. The account plan is updated every quarter.

Stage 2 · Weeks 4 to 13 · Engine 1

Learn: Flight 1

Six independent rounds on the ICP layer, eighteen ads. Each round changes one element and keeps everything else at the house version, so it answers one question about the buyer. The answers go into the learning log, which feeds Flight 2.

Rounds 1 and 2

Cold ICP audience, split in two halves. Objective: engagement. CTA: Learn more.

Rounds 3 and 4

ICP engagers: ad, video and document engagers, site visitors, company page visitors, 90 days. Objective: engagement and website visits. If a half falls below 300, run the two rounds one after the other on the full pool (about three weeks more).

Rounds 5 and 6

Engaged pool and key page visitors, 30 days, with LinkedIn lead forms. The qualification rule and the same-day handoff (see Run) go live with the first lead form, around week 10. If a half falls below 300, run the two rounds one after the other on the full pool and move Build back by about three weeks.

Stage 3 · Weeks 12 to 14

Build: Flight 2

One core message built from everything Flight 1 learned: the lever, the offer, the reason to believe, the format, the ask and the situation that worked. It is written once per buying role, keeps the industry and situation in the copy and never names the account. The expert post is refreshed with the same message.

Gate to Run
  • Core set approved: one message per role and the refreshed expert post
  • Every claim, offer and promise checked against the company's own pages
  • Role audiences built for test accounts, each clearing 300 members
Stage 4 · Week 14 to window end · Engine 2

Run

One loop for one full sales cycle (at least 90 days). The core set goes to the test arm only; the control arm gets the always-on post and normal sales activity. The rule decides when sales steps in, the standup moves the accounts, and the scorecard shows both arms every month.

The ABM layer

Broad ABM · test arm

The committee, by role

Company lists of test accounts (tier 1 clustered with test-arm or non-target accounts, never control), with function and seniority layered per role. Cells C1 to C3. Engagers are excluded, so nobody sits in two campaigns. Objective: engagement and website visits.

Warm ABM · test arm

People who engaged

Engagers at test accounts, pooled by industry to clear 300, shown the Flight 1 winning ask with a lead form. Accounts too small to serve go to sales as a list.

Opportunity stage · test arm

The wider committee

When an opportunity opens, the ads widen to validation and consensus: case studies, the C4 document for IT, security and procurement, and the expert post.

Always on · everyone in the ICP

Expert post (C5)

Starts in week 4 with the house message and runs to the end, to the whole ICP, both arms included, at a low steady budget. Build refreshes it with the core message. It keeps the brand in memory for accounts that are not in market yet.

Keep the control clean. Check weekly that control accounts are excluded from every ABM ad set and sales play. Contamination makes the result look smaller; quietly moving accounts out of the control makes it look bigger. Both make it useless.

Qualify and hand off

Live since the first lead form in Learn. The rule was agreed in Set up and does not change during the window.

Qualification rule

When an account becomes an MQA

Simple rule: two or more people from the same account show a signal within two weeks.

Scored rule: [60] engagement minutes in the last [90] days from at least [3] roles, one of them [VP] or above.

Act now: [a director requests a meeting; two people view the demo].

Excluded: partners, competitors, poor fit.

Signals: page visits and post engagement from the account, visits to [pricing, demo, key pages], event registrations, a new executive in a relevant role, third-party intent.

Minutes: page visit [1], ad engagement [2], email reply [2], download [10], event [20 to 30], sales meeting [30].

Outreach

Three messages per role

  • After the accept: context and one question about their problem
  • A few days later: the strongest proof for that role
  • A week later: a small next step or a graceful exit
  • Tier 1 notes written by a person; never two senders on one stakeholder
  • Meeting booked: pause the account's other sequences. Quiet after the sequence: back to warm-up
Handoff

Same day, four points

  • Who replied, their role and what they said
  • Which signal or content moved them
  • Who else at the account engaged, and how
  • The agreed next step and its date

Logged in the CRM on the company record.

Rhythm

GTM standup

Every one or two weeks, 5 to 10 minutes per account owner: what happened, the top 3 to 5 accounts to move, 2 opportunities to accelerate, who does what, blockers. Alerts by email or Slack between standups.

Monthly scorecard, from week 4

Coverage and engagement move in weeks; pipeline moves in quarters. Until Run starts, both arms show the ICP layer only, which is the check that the split is balanced.

Stage 5 · Window end, about week 27

Readout and scale

The test arm against the control arm on opportunity rate and pipeline per account, then one decision. Tier 1 is reported on its own.

The decision at window end

Scale
  • Test arm clearly ahead on opportunity rate or pipeline per account
  • Sales confirms the conversations
  • Extend the ABM layer to the control arm and the next tier
Adjust
  • Engagement up, pipeline flat
  • The ads work; the handoff, the offer or the rule does not
  • Fix qualification and handoff, keep the split, run a second window
Stop or redesign
  • No difference after a full window
  • Go back to the learning log and the account list
  • Run a new Flight 1 round where the gap is
Optional

Expansion round

The bow-tie: after closed-won, the same method grows existing accounts. Three product cards, one variable (the next product), read on meetings with current clients. It runs on the client list with its own read, so it can start at any time and does not feed the learning log or the core set.

Include when the client sells several products and the client list clears 300 matched members in the relevant roles. Below that, expansion runs as a sales and email motion, outside LinkedIn.
